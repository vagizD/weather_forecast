THE CHALLENGE

Why 14-Day Forecasting Fails

Autoregressive compounding and the mathematical danger of MSE loss

🌪️
1. Autoregressive Compounding
Step-by-step prediction ($t \to t+1 \to t+2$) compounds errors exponentially. By Day 3–4, trajectories diverge into unphysical chaos.
📉
2. The MSE Trap (Diurnal Collapse)
MSE heavily penalizes variance. Under uncertainty, it forces models to predict the conditional mean $\to$ flat line! Even for residuals, MSE flattens the wave to zero!
🎯 Slide Objective

Explain the two structural failure modes: autoregressive compounding and conditional mean flattening under MSE loss.

🗣️ Speaker Talking Points

"Why is 14-day weather forecasting so notoriously difficult? First, autoregression fails: predicting hour-by-hour compounds errors exponentially by day 3. Second, the MSE trap: Mean Squared Error heavily penalizes any phase or amplitude mismatch. Under multi-week uncertainty, minimizing MSE mathematically forces the model to hedge its bets by predicting the conditional mean. The mean of an oscillating sine wave is a flat line—the daily 20-degree diurnal temperature cycle collapses into a flatline! Even if predicting residuals, MSE flattens the residual to zero."

🗣️ Speaker Talking Points

OPTIMIZATION OBJECTIVE

Residual Formulation with L1 Loss

Decomposing predictions into climatology + neural residual anomalies using MAE

$$\widehat{y}(t) = y_{\text{clim}}(t) + \widehat{r}_\theta(t), \qquad \min_\theta \frac{1}{336}\sum_{h=1}^{336} \left| \widehat{y}_{t+h} - y_{t+h} \right|$$
1. Climatological Prior $y_{\text{clim}}(t)$
Zero-parameter empirical 26-year average for each (Day of Year, Hour). Guarantees realistic diurnal amplitude.
2. Why L1 (MAE) Over L2 (MSE)
L1 loss tracks median trajectory without over-penalizing phase variance. Prevents the residual from collapsing to zero!
🎯 Slide Objective

Explain the residual decomposition formulation and why L1/MAE loss is mathematically superior to MSE.

🗣️ Speaker Talking Points

"Our objective decomposes temperature into historical climatology plus a learned residual anomaly, trained using L1 Mean Absolute Error rather than MSE. Why L1? Because L1 optimizes the conditional median rather than the conditional mean. It provides constant gradient magnitude regardless of error size, preventing the network from hedging and flattening its residual oscillations to zero."

APPROACH 2: DEEP LEARNING

Deep Learning: Core Intuition

Two architectures designed to overcome weather forecasting challenges

🧩
PatchTST: Day-by-Day Matching
• Daily Patches: Groups 24 hours into a single 'token'. Compares day-to-day weather patterns instead of noisy hourly steps.
• Independent Channels: Processes temperature, wind, and pressure separately so wind gusts don't corrupt thermal trends.
⚡
DenseNet: The Calendar Highway
• Direct Calendar Injection: Feeds month, hour, and year embeddings directly into every layer.
• No Forgotten Context: Prevents deep layers from losing temporal context, forcing embeddings to encode true seasonal warming.
🎯 Slide Objective

Explain the intuitive mechanisms of PatchTST and DenseNet without confusing deep learning jargon.

🗣️ Speaker Talking Points

"For deep learning, we avoided black-box complexity and focused on two intuitive architectures: PatchTST slices the time series into 24-hour daily patches. Instead of getting lost in hourly noise, it matches day-to-day synoptic weather systems, keeping physical variables independent. DenseNet introduces a 'Calendar Highway': it injects month and hour embeddings into every single layer, ensuring the network never loses track of seasonal and decadal climate shifts."

KEY TAKEAWAYS

Robustness & Validation Strategy

Key conclusions for multi-week operational weather forecasting

🛡️
1. Robustness Beats Complexity
Constrained, regularized models (Multi-output Ridge, regularized PatchTST) avoid overfitting to chaotic micro-fluctuations and generalize better.
🎯
2. Season-Aligned Validation
Spring validation gave noisy signals due to frontal turbulence. Aligning validation with target season (e.g., September 2025 or late summer) is crucial for model selection.
⚓
3. Climatological Anchor
Predicting residual anomalies over historical climatology guarantees physically realistic diurnal oscillations even when 14-day predictability decays.
🎯 Slide Objective

Conclude with key takeaways focused on model robustness, season-aligned validation, and climatological priors.

🗣️ Speaker Talking Points

"To conclude: First, model robustness is key to reducing overfitting. Highly constrained models like Ridge and regularized PatchTST outperform unconstrained architectures on long horizons. Second, validation alignment is critical: using Spring data gave misleadingly high error due to jet stream turbulence; validating on season-aligned periods like September 2025 or late summer gives far better guidance for operational deployment. Third, residual climatology provides an essential physical anchor. Thank you, and we welcome any questions."
